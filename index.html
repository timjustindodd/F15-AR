Flight 15 ship return – what you're looking at

The path is APPROXIMATE. SpaceX has not published coordinates for the return ground track. This is a reconstruction, not telemetry.

What it's based on

  • Approach direction – SpaceX, "Returning Starship to the Launch Site" (Nov 7, 2026): Starship reenters over the Pacific, flies over sparsely populated northern Mexico and southern Texas, "approaches Starbase from the west", weaves around the Rio Grande Valley cities, passes north of South Padre Island and out over the Gulf, then makes a final turn south toward Starbase, bellyflops toward the Pad 2 tower, lights 3 Raptors, flips, and is caught. spacex.com/updates
  • Timeline – spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15: entry T+22:10:46, transonic T+22:29:34, subsonic T+22:30:13, landing burn T+22:32:16, flip T+22:32:18, 3→2 engines T+22:32:26, 2→1 T+22:32:33, catch T+22:32:34. Liftoff was 4:47:36 PM CT Nov 13, so the default catch time is 3:20:10 PM CT, Sat Nov 14 (editable below).
  • Altitude/speed anchors – Flight 14 webcast telemetry: transonic at ~20 km and ~1,062 km/h, "subsonic" callout at ~16 km. Everything else (the hypersonic part, the exact turn, the bellyflop glide) is an engineering guess with smooth interpolation.
  • Map – Gulf/South Padre coastline and Rio Grande from OpenStreetMap (simplified); the Laguna Madre mainland shore is hand-approximated.
  • Rocket Ranch Outpost – approximate: placed on the Rio Grande 3.7 mi (Rocket Ranch's own straight-line figure) from the tower, ~25.958 N, 97.197 W. The campground (43742 Palmito Hill Rd) is ~25.947 N, 97.279 W.

Limits

  • Camera + motion sensors need HTTPS (or localhost). Opened as a file:// page, only the 3D view works.
  • iPhone Safari has no WebXR AR, so on iOS this uses camera passthrough + compass (iOS 13+ asks for Motion permission). Android Chrome uses WebXR immersive-ar when supported.
  • Compass accuracy varies (±5–15° is normal; metal and cars make it worse). Point at the Starbase tower and tap Align to tower to fix heading. Elevation (tilt) is more reliable than heading.
  • Phone camera field of view varies; adjust FOV if the tower/horizon don't line up.
  • Earth curvature is included in az/el; atmospheric refraction is not.
